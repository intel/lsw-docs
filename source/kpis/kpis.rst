KPI Definitions
===============

Abstract KPIs
-------------

Abstract KPIs are not bound to specific measurement tools and represent general latency criteria applicable across different measurement approaches.

**Maximum Value**
   Maximal latency spike duration.

**Pass or Fail**
   Binary assessment indicating whether the maximum value exceeds latency threshold.

**Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF)**
   Statistical measure of the average operational time between latency spikes exceeding latency thresholds.

   Rough approximation:

   .. math::

      MTBF = \frac{Total\ Operating\ Time}{Number\ of\ Failures}

**CPU Availability**
   The percentage of runtime that was free from noise related latencies.

**Aggregate Percentage**
   The percentage of samples at or below a specified latency threshold across all threads.

   .. math::

      Aggregate\ Percentage = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} Samples_{\leq threshold,i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} Total\_Samples_i} \times 100\%

   where :math:`n` is the number of threads, :math:`Samples_{\leq threshold,i}` is the count of samples at or below the threshold for thread :math:`i`, and :math:`Total\_Samples_i` is the total number of samples for thread :math:`i`.

This section presents details regarding the specific metrics that are to be measured along with definitions for each of the measurement tools of interest.
For example, the average, and maximum latency reported by cyclictest, the maximum noise reported by hwnoise, and so forth.
This list of metrics distinguishes between the values reported directly by each of the corresponding tools, which are labeled as "raw" metrics, along with metrics that are calculated based upon these raw metrics, which are labeled as "derived" metrics.
An example of a raw metric would be the maximum latency reported by cyclictest.
An example of a derived metric would be the percentage of samples that are at or below a latency threshold of 10 μs, which are calculated based on the histogram generated by cyclictest.
Where applicable, units are also included with the associated measurement.
Several metrics are not KPIs because they depend on test duration.
